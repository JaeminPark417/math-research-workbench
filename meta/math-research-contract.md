# Reviewing and resuming a mathematical claim

Use these rules when a project returns to a claim, changes its statement, or relies on it in another proof. Apply them in the project's existing notes or records. A short question needs no new form.

1. **Identify the exact claim.** Keep the statement as supplied, with its hypotheses, quantifiers, definitions and source version. Record which lemma is used at which step. A changed domain or quantifier creates a proposed revision; retain the old wording and evidence.
2. **Keep the scope of a counterexample.** Give the witness, check every hypothesis and show exactly which conclusion fails. Record why the attempted route failed and the condition that would warrant retrying it. If the witness only refutes a stronger statement or one proof step, keep that distinction.
3. **Recheck what changed.** Leave the old review attached to the old version. Identify the changed proof steps and their dependents. If a revision becomes canonical, flag affected uses for recheck. While it is proposed, describe those possible effects in the proposal.
4. **Describe what was checked and by whom.** A fresh solution or counterexample search receives the exact problem and permitted sources without previous attempts. An independent proof audit receives the complete candidate proof, its exact target and versioned dependencies, without the originating conversation or prior verdicts. Record the actual inputs and any exposure. Before relying on a load-bearing proof or counterexample, seek an independent check; if none is available, keep its review pending. Report the inputs and scope of computations, literature and observed formal checks.
5. **Leave a usable next step.** From a new conversation, the researcher should be able to find the exact target and version, the last material attempt and its evidence, the unresolved obligation, and one action that would advance it. Point to the durable evidence and the existing owner of that action. Check the source version again before using a past review. When only a status report is requested, summarize and stop.
6. **Record the researcher's decision.** Keep proposed repairs, completed tasks, review outcomes and mathematical decisions distinct. Only the researcher confirms that an encoding matches the intended statement, promotes a claim, closes a gap or chooses a version for circulation. Record the scope and version of the decision.

For example, v1 asks whether every bounded continuous real function on (0,1) attains its maximum. For f(x) = x, the supremum is 1 and no point attains it. In proposed v2 the domain is [0,1]. The old counterexample does not refute v2, and checking that f(x) = x now has a maximum does not prove the universal statement. A good next step is to check that the continuous image of the nonempty compact interval is compact and therefore contains its supremum.
